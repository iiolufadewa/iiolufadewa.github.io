Two questions, one method

I model how things move through people —
distress, pathogens, and care.

Whether the signal is a virus on a hospital surface or the words someone uses for despair, the mathematics of transmission is the same. My research turns that idea into tools clinicians and communities can actually use.

01

Computational psychiatry

Machine learning & NLP for mental health

I build language models and deep-learning systems that recognise early signals of psychological distress — and conversational agents that offer stigma-free, culturally grounded support in the languages people actually speak, including Nigerian Pidgin, Hausa and Yoruba.

NLPFine-tuned LLMsSentiment analysisConversational AI
02

Network epidemiology

Network analysis & mathematical modelling

I use contact networks and compartmental models to map how infections spread, find the superspreaders and surfaces that drive outbreaks, and forecast trajectories — so that limited resources reach the places and moments that matter most.

Network analysisSIR / SEIRSuperspreader detectionML forecasting

Where it points

Research that stays close to the ground.

Digital mental health equity

Democratising psychiatric support for young people in slums, rural areas and displacement camps — where stigma is high and clinicians are few.

Outbreak & infection dynamics

Modelling healthcare-associated infections, fomite transmission and community outbreaks to inform faster, fairer public-health response.

Global burden & policy

Contributing to the Global Burden of Disease Study and advising WHO and the Lancet Commission to keep the Global South in the evidence base.

AI anchored in African context

Insisting that models built for African communities be trained, validated and governed with those communities — not imported wholesale.

Data equity engines

Building pipelines that give low-resource settings automated, verified access to the medical and psychological data they need.

Mentorship & capacity

Training the next generation of African physician-scientists through SRHIN’s research academy — from first abstract to first Lancet paper.

Where people live should not determine whether they live.
— The premise behind everything I build

I grew up more than three kilometres from the nearest quality clinic. That distance — measured in kilometres, in language, in data that never gets collected — is the problem my work exists to close. Computation is simply the fastest lever I have found for closing it at scale.

Let’s build something that reaches further.

I welcome collaborations with neuro-AI and computational-health labs, digital-health ventures, academic institutions and multilateral global-health organisations.
