Research programme

Computation in service of the last mile.

My research sits where clinical medicine, artificial intelligence and global-health policy meet. It runs along two lines that share a single mathematics — the mathematics of how things move through a population — and a single goal: reaching the people conventional systems reach last.

Pillar 01 · Computational psychiatry

Machine learning & NLP for mental health.

Most mental-health AI is trained on English, on the wealthy, and on the clinic. I work on the inverse problem: detecting and responding to distress in the languages, idioms and settings of the Global South, where the treatment gap is widest and stigma is highest.

Distress in its own language

Fine-tuning large language models to recognise depression and anxiety in Nigerian Pidgin, Hausa and Yoruba — not by translating Western instruments, but by learning how communities actually express suffering.

Stigma-free conversational agents

Building chatbots and screening tools that let people seek help privately, at any hour, without the shame that keeps so many away from a clinic door.

Behavioural pattern analytics

Using sequence mining and semantic understanding to map how community mental health shifts over time — turning scattered signals into something a programme can act on.

Pillar 02 · Network epidemiology

Network analysis & mathematical modelling.

An outbreak is a graph before it is a headline. My modelling work treats people, places and surfaces as nodes in a transmission network, so that the few interventions a stretched system can afford land on the edges that matter.

Superspreader detection

Finding the drivers of hospital-acquired infection

Network models that separate the fomites, patients and health workers most responsible for spreading infection inside a hospital — so infection control targets the real hubs rather than everything at once.

Forecasting & surveillance

Predicting incidence before it peaks

Compartmental (SIR / SEIR) and machine-learning models for outbreak trajectories and ICU infection risk, built to run on the sparse, imperfect data real health systems have — not the clean data papers assume.

Flagship projects

A community-led mental-health intervention for young internally displaced persons, delivered by trained lay counsellors and now running in Nigeria and Sierra Leone. Peer-reviewed evaluations show measurable reductions in depression and anxiety severity.
