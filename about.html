The short version

Dr. Isaac Iyinoluwa Olufadewa is a medical doctor and computational scientist working at the intersection of clinical medicine, artificial intelligence and global-health policy. His research builds scalable digital-health frameworks — using machine learning, natural language processing, network analysis and mathematical modelling — to democratise mental-health support, strengthen infectious-disease response, and dismantle the stigma and distance that keep care from the people who need it most across the Global South.

He is the Founder and Executive Director of the Slum and Rural Health Initiative (SRHIN), and co-founder of SimbiHealth, an AI-driven mental- and sexual-health platform. His applied and academic work — more than 100 peer-reviewed publications, including contributions to The Lancet and the Global Burden of Disease Study — has been cited over 12,000 times.

How he got here

Isaac grew up in a rural community where the nearest quality health centre was more than three kilometres away, and where the absence of health information carried a visible human cost. That experience became the thesis of his career: that geography, language and missing data should never determine who gets to be well.

He earned his medical degree (MBBS) at the University of Ibadan, Nigeria’s premier university, then completed a postgraduate degree in reproductive health as a Pan African University Scholar on a full African Union Commission scholarship. He went on to a PhD in computational epidemiology in the United States, where his doctoral work applied network analysis and machine learning to healthcare-associated infections. Along the way he served as a US CDC HIRe Modeling Fellow, focused on infectious-disease modelling.

Today he advises global institutions — as a member of a WHO Technical Working Group and a Task Force Expert with the Lancet COVID-19 Commission — while continuing to build and evaluate tools on the ground with SRHIN, including the BraveHeart community mental-health programme now running in Nigeria and Sierra Leone.

Beyond the work

Isaac is a committed mentor and community builder — he rejuvenated The Literary and Debating Society at the University of Ibadan and has co-founded several health platforms, including the SRHIN Research Academy and the HEAL Society. He has represented his work in more than fourteen countries across four continents, and remains most energised by the moment a locally built tool first reaches someone a health system had written off.
