About

I am a medical doctor and computational scientist working at the intersection of clinical medicine, artificial intelligence and global health.

I am the Founder and Executive Director of the Slum and Rural Health Initiative (SRHIN), where research, technology and community implementation come together to address health challenges in underserved settings.

My work includes machine learning, natural language processing, network analysis and mathematical modelling. Across these areas, the focus is practical: developing evidence and tools that can reach people who are often last to benefit from conventional health systems.

Background

I earned my MBBS at the University of Ibadan and later completed postgraduate training in reproductive health as a Pan African University Scholar. I subsequently completed a PhD in computational epidemiology in the United States, applying network analysis and machine learning to healthcare-associated infections.

I have also served as a US CDC HIRe Modeling Fellow and have contributed to international global-health initiatives, including work associated with the WHO and the Lancet COVID-19 Commission.
